== hook | ground: dark
Every *key*

== answer | ground: dark
So how is that
*possible?*
Because the keys
you *see*
are not the keys
you *touch,*
and the ones you touch
change *size*
while you *type.*
== zone | ground: dark
Under every *letter*
there's an
*invisible* zone,
and that zone
is what actually
*catches* your tap.
== tim | ground: dark
Imagine you're
*texting* a friend
and you've just typed
*T, I, M.*
Your iPhone already
has a *guess*

because *"time"* / is a word
and *"timw"* is not.
== grow | ground: dark
So before your thumb
even comes *down,*
the invisible zone
around the *E*
gets *bigger,*
and the zones around it
get *squeezed.*
== same | ground: dark
On the screen,
*nothing* moves.
Every key looks
exactly the *same* size.
But if your thumb
lands a little *off,*
*halfway* between
the E and the *W,*
the iPhone
gives it to the *E.*
== again | ground: dark
Then you type
the *next* letter
and every zone
changes *again,*
for every *single* letter
you type.
== first | ground: dark
Apple built this

was only *320* / pixels wide.
*Ken Kocienda,*
the engineer who made
that *keyboard,*

*patented* it.
== popup | ground: dark
And it comes with
a *second* trick.
Your thumb *hides*
the key you're pressing,

the letter *pops up*
above your *finger,*
so you can see
what you actually *hit.*
== close | ground: dark
So you're not
that *accurate*

Your keyboard
is quietly *moving*
the *target*
